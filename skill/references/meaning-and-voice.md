# Meaning and voice

Use this reference while preparing a draft. It separates what must remain true from the choices that
make the writing belong to a particular person.

## Meaning contract

Record this internally before drafting or rewriting:

| Field | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Point | What one thing must the reader understand? |
| Evidence | Which facts, names, numbers and quotations support it? |
| Certainty | What is known, inferred, expected or merely possible? |
| Boundaries | Which caveats or exclusions materially limit the claim? |
| Ask | What should the reader think, decide or do next? |
| Posture | Is the writer informing, disagreeing, reassuring, apologising, persuading or escalating? |
| Protected material | What wording or content must remain unchanged? |
| Forbidden additions | What must not be invented or implied? |

After drafting, compare the result with every row. Style never outranks fidelity.

## Reader model

Channel describes the container. Relationship determines the writing.

Assess:

- **Relationship:** stranger, customer, peer, manager, direct report, regulator, investor or public.
- **Power:** who can approve, refuse, delay or impose a cost?
- **Trust:** unearned, developing or established.
- **Context:** what does the reader already know?
- **State:** calm, anxious, sceptical, frustrated, excited or time-poor.
- **Resistance:** what objection or fear will appear first?
- **Stakes:** how costly is ambiguity or error?
- **Action:** what should happen after reading?

Use these answers to decide how much context, warmth, directness and evidence the piece needs.

## Voice evidence hierarchy

Use evidence in this order:

1. The user's edits to a draft for the same channel and relationship.
2. Raw human samples from the same channel and relationship.
3. Raw samples from the same channel.
4. Confirmed patterns across several channels.
5. The user's stated convictions and absolute rules.
6. Provisional profile observations.
7. Engine defaults.

Downweight heavily polished, ghostwritten or model-assisted samples. Treat a single occurrence as an
observation, not a rule. Record negative examples because they define the edge of the voice.

## Evidence labels

- **Confirmed:** repeated in at least three independent examples or explicitly stated by the user.
- **Supported:** appears in two examples or one example plus user confirmation.
- **Provisional:** appears once or was inferred during calibration.
- **Unknown:** no direct evidence for this channel or relationship.

When evidence is unknown, avoid importing conspicuous habits from another channel. Clean and
conservative is better than confidently wrong.

## What voice actually includes

Surface features matter, but they are the least durable layer. Model these layers from strongest to
weakest:

1. **Selection:** what the writer believes is worth mentioning.
2. **Judgement:** what they think matters, failed, worked or remains uncertain.
3. **Relationship:** how they position themselves relative to the reader.
4. **Structure:** the order in which they reveal the point, evidence, objection and ask.
5. **Cadence:** sentence and paragraph movement.
6. **Diction and punctuation:** repeated wording and surface habits.

If a draft copies cadence and phrases but makes decisions the user would not make, it has missed the
voice.

## Contrastive calibration

When calibrating from limited evidence, offer up to three genuinely different versions:

- **Close:** minimal intervention, source structure mostly preserved.
- **Direct:** point and ask moved forward, padding cut.
- **Conversational:** warmer and closer to speech without becoming casual by default.

Ask which is closest and what the user would change. Record the reason behind the preference. The
choice between versions is more useful than agreement with abstract words such as “clear” or “human”.

## Transformation boundaries

| Mode | May change | Must preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light edit | errors, friction, obvious tells | wording choices, order, meaning |
| Voice rewrite | sentences, flow, emphasis | argument, evidence, certainty, ask |
| Editorial reconstruction | argument order, framing, missing questions | supplied facts, protected wording, user authority |

Move to a stronger mode only when the request authorises it or a weaker mode cannot produce useful
writing.
